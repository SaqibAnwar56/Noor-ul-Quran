About Noor-ul-Quran

Noor-ul-Quran is a free online Qur'an, made by Saqib Anwar, built to be a single, calm place for daily reading and remembrance. Read the complete Qur'an by all 114 Surahs or in the traditional 30 Paras (Juz), each shown with its Arabic name, Arabic script, English translation, and recitation audio. Alongside the Qur'an you'll find the essentials Muslims turn to every day: Durood Paak (Salat al-Ibrahimiyyah), Ayat-ul-Kursi, the Four Quls, the 99 Names of Allah (Asma-ul-Husna) — plus a Tasbih counter and a place to save your favourite verses. The homepage is open to everyone; a free sign-up unlocks the Qur'an reader and the rest of the site.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Durood Paak?

Durood Paak, also called Durood-e-Ibrahim or Salat al-Ibrahimiyyah, is the blessing the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught his companions to recite upon him. It is recited in the Tashahhud of every Salah, after the Adhan, on Fridays, and whenever his name is mentioned.

What is Ayat-ul-Kursi?

Ayat-ul-Kursi is verse 255 of Surah Al-Baqarah, known as the Verse of the Throne. It is one of the most widely recited verses of the Qur'an, commonly read after each prayer and before sleeping.

What are the 4 Quls?

The Four Quls are Surah Al-Kafirun, Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq, and An-Nas — four short Surahs that each open with the word "Qul" (Say), commonly recited together for daily protection.

Do I need to sign up to use Noor-ul-Quran?

The homepage is open to everyone. A free sign-up or log-in is required to open the Qur'an reader, Durood Paak, Ayat-ul-Kursi, the Four Quls, and the other content sections.

Durood Paak

Durood Paak — also known as Durood-e-Ibrahim, Durood Sharif, or Salat al-Ibrahimiyyah — is the blessing the Prophet ﷺ himself taught his companions to recite upon him. It is recited in the Tashahhud of every Salah, after the Adhan, on Fridays (Jumu'ah), and any time his name ﷺ is mentioned.

O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and upon the family of Muhammad, as You sent blessings upon Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim; indeed You are Praiseworthy, Glorious. O Allah, bless Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You blessed Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim; indeed You are Praiseworthy, Glorious.

Reported in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, from the hadith of Ka'b ibn 'Ujrah (may Allah be pleased with him). Tap the play button to hear it recited aloud.

Why Muslims recite Durood Paak

The Qur'an itself instructs believers to send blessings on the Prophet ﷺ: "Indeed, Allah confers blessing upon the Prophet, and His angels [ask Him to do so]. O you who have believed, ask [Allah to confer] blessing upon him and ask [Allah to grant him] peace." (Qur'an 33:56). The Prophet ﷺ also said that whoever sends one blessing upon him, Allah sends ten blessings upon them in return (Sahih Muslim). For this reason Durood Sharif is recited daily — in prayer, after Adhan, on Fridays, and whenever his blessed name is mentioned.
